# MEMORANDUM — COPPA §312.2 "Disclosure" and the Availability of Email-Plus Consent for Closed Family-Group Visibility

**Re:** RoadTrip Royale — whether intra-family display of a child's username, avatar, and gameplay data is "disclosure" under 16 CFR §312.2, foreclosing the email-plus consent method of §312.5(b)(2)(viii)

**Regulatory analysis prepared to brief counsel; not legal advice.**

## Executive Summary

Email-plus survives the 2025 amendments, recodified at §312.5(b)(2)(viii) (a parallel "text plus" was added at (b)(2)(ix)); both are available only to "an operator that does not 'disclose' (as defined by §312.2) children's personal information" (verified rule text). On the established facts, my opinion is that RoadTrip Royale can use email-plus, and I rate the closed-group reading **defensible-but-untested**. Critically, the position is strongest not on the ground the question emphasizes (that family members are not "third parties") but on a logically prior ground: **nothing shown to family members is itself "personal information" under §312.2**. The username does not function as online contact information (no contact surface exists), the avatar is a stock cartoon, and gameplay data is PI only when combined with an enumerated identifier — which is never displayed. The one unambiguous item of child PI, the Firebase uid, flows only to the operator and its service provider, a flow the disclosure definition expressly excludes. The pure "family members aren't third parties" argument, standing alone, is textually strained (weak-to-defensible); the composite position is defensible. The planned upgrade to a high-assurance method before any cross-family visibility is exactly the right trigger line.

## 1. §312.2 "Disclose or disclosure" Applied

**Prong (2) — public availability: inapplicable, with high confidence.** Prong (2) reaches "[m]aking personal information … publicly available in identifiable form by any means," with examples — public posting, personal home page, pen pal service, email service, message board, chat room (verified text). Every example involves exposure beyond an operator-controlled, identity-verified set of recipients. Here, visibility is server-enforced to captain-approved family members; children are non-searchable; there is no chat, no free text, no profiles visible outside the family. Nothing is "publicly available." FTC staff guidance confirms prong (2) is the prong doing the work for user-facing social features (FAQ I.9, discussing social networks, chat rooms, message boards as prong-(2) cases).

**Prong (1) — release to a third party.** Prong (1) covers "[t]he release of personal information … in identifiable form for any purpose, except where an operator provides such information to a person who provides support for the internal operations." "Release" means "the sharing, selling, renting, or transfer of personal information to any third party"; "third party" is "any person" who is neither an operator with respect to the data nor a support-for-internal-operations provider (verified text). Read literally, a grandmother viewing the child's scores is a "person" fitting neither exclusion, and on-screen display is arguably "sharing." That is the reading's soft spot, and it should be conceded rather than argued around.

Two responses. *First and dispositive if accepted:* prong (1) is only triggered by release of **personal information** — and, as Section 2 shows, the displayed bundle contains none. *Second, structural:* if prong (1) reached any user-facing display, prong (2) would be surplusage — every public posting is also "sharing" with third parties. The two-prong architecture, and the Rule's history, treat user-facing exposure as prong (2)'s domain (and prong (2) demands publicity), while prong (1) targets transfers of data out of the operator's control to other entities. The 2025 SBP's entire discussion of third-party release concerns advertisers, data clean rooms, marketing partnerships, and platform-to-publisher data flows (90 FR 16918 at 16949-50 & n.388) — never co-users of a closed feature.

**Does the coexistence of high-assurance methods imply the condition targets disclosure to the world?** Partially. The sliding scale's original rationale was that "internal uses of information … presented less risk than external disclosures of the information to third parties or through public postings" (1999 SBP, 64 FR 59888 at 59901, quoted at 78 FR 3972 n.239; verified). That dichotomy — internal use versus *external* transfer or *public* posting — supports treating parent-scoped intra-service display as the low-risk side of the line. But the 2025 SBP restates the rationale as impersonation risk: email/text-plus "carry a higher risk of a child impersonating a parent" (90 FR 16918 at 16955-56; verified). That cuts the other way: a forged consent would also corrupt the family-membership control that makes the group safe. The coexistence argument is legitimate support, not a trump.

## 2. Is What's Displayed Even PI?

**Username.** PI includes "[a] screen or user name where it functions in the same manner as online contact information" — i.e., "permits direct contact with a person online" (§312.2, verified). With zero contact surfaces (no chat, DMs, comments, or free text), non-searchability, rejection of inbound friend requests, and server-side rejection of email/phone-shaped usernames, the username is a display label, not a contact vector. The 2025 SBP squarely confirms the test: the Commission *declined* to expand the definition to names that "do not allow one user to contact another user through the operator's website or online service," warning that expansion would perversely force more data collection (90 FR 16918 at 16929-30; verified). One caution the SBP adds: a screen name "combined with other personal information" becomes PI under the combination paragraph (now ¶(11)). In the operator's records — username linked to uid — the record as held is PI. But what is *released* to family members contains no enumerated identifier, so the released bundle does not satisfy ¶(11) on its own terms. This is the aggressive regulator's best foothold (see Section 4).

**Avatar.** A fixed cartoon-catalog avatar is not "[a] photograph, video, or audio file … contain[ing] a child's image or voice" (¶(8)). The 2025 SBP declined to treat even avatars *generated from a child's image* as PI (90 FR 16918 at 16930-31; verified). Not PI, high confidence.

**Gameplay data.** Plate discoveries and scores are PI only via ¶(11) — "combine[d] with an identifier described in this definition." Displayed alongside a non-PI username only, they are not.

**Firebase uid — separately.** The uid is PI per se (¶(7), persistent identifier). Two distinct roles for "support for the internal operations" (SFIO): (a) the §312.5(c)(7) *consent exception* (persistent identifier + no other PI + SFIO-only) is unavailable post-consent and unnecessary — the operator obtains VPC anyway; (b) the SFIO carve-outs in the disclosure and third-party definitions are what matter for email-plus: providing the uid and synced data to Firebase/Google as the operator's service provider is *not* disclosure, provided Google is contractually confined to providing the service (§312.2 prong (1) exception; "third party" ¶(2)) and §312.8(c) written assurances exist. The operator's own internal *use* of the uid (auth, sync) is use, not disclosure — and note the condition on email-plus is textually "does not disclose," not "uses only for internal operations." Practitioner commentary sometimes asserts email-plus forbids leaderboards or any non-SFIO use outright; that gloss overreads the rule text, which conditions the method on non-disclosure alone (the 1999-2013 history even lists internal marketing, fan clubs, and sweepstakes as compatible "internal uses," 78 FR 3972 n.239).

## 3. The 2025 Amendments, SBP, and Enforcement Landscape

Verified from the amended rule and 2025 SBP: (i) email-plus retained and renumbered (b)(2)(vi)→(viii); text-plus added at (b)(2)(ix), both non-disclosure-conditioned; (ii) new §312.5(a)(2) requires *separate* consent for non-integral third-party disclosures — the SBP discussion is entirely about commercial recipients (advertisers, AI training, platform/publisher flows), not co-users; (iii) screen-name and avatar expansions declined (above); (iv) new "mixed audience" definition requires neutral age screening — relevant to the app's posture; (v) new mandatory written security program (§312.8(b)) and written retention policy (§312.10). **Nothing in the amended text or SBP addresses intra-family or closed-group visibility; I found no FTC enforcement action or guidance either blessing or condemning parent-controlled family-group display.** *United States v. Microsoft* (Xbox, 2023) — the closest family-account ecosystem — turned on pre-consent collection/retention and notice failures, not family visibility; *Epic Games* (2022) targeted default-on communication with strangers, the inverse of this design. Treat the silence as untested ground, not safety.

## 4. Opinion

**Rating: defensible-but-untested** (composite). The layered position — (1) no PI is displayed to family members; (2) the uid goes only to a SFIO service provider; (3) prong (2) requires publicity that never occurs; (4) structurally, prong (1) was not built for intra-service co-user display — is a position I would be comfortable defending to FTC staff, particularly given the 2025 SBP's explicit hostility to expanding the screen-name definition. Standing alone, the pure closed-group claim ("captain-approved family members are not 'third parties'") is **weak-to-defensible**: the definitions are literal and broad, and I would not lead with it.

**Best argument against, stated fairly:** The username, avatar, and scores are collected combined with the uid, making the record PI under ¶(11) as collected and maintained; displaying part of that record to persons who can trivially identify the child (his own family) is a "release of personal information … in identifiable form" to "third parties" under the plain text. On that view the operator "discloses," and email-plus — whose 2025-restated rationale is precisely that a child can forge it, corrupting the parental controls the closed group depends on — is foreclosed. This is a coherent, textually grounded position a motivated regulator could take.

**Single fact change that flips the rating:** any visibility of the child's username/avatar/scores outside the parent-controlled family — cross-family leaderboards, child friends, searchability — flips this to **foreclosed**: display to strangers of a persistent handle is at minimum a ¶(11) release and likely re-characterizes the username itself. The operator's planned trigger — high-assurance method (card transaction or ID verification, §312.5(b)(2)(ii)/(v)/(vii)) *before* enabling any such feature — is exactly the right line. Equally load-bearing: adding *any* contact surface keyed to the username (chat, mentions, DMs) makes the username PI under ¶(4) immediately, independent of visibility scope.

## 5. Questions for Counsel (ordered by importance)

1. Does counsel concur that the released bundle (non-contact username + stock avatar + scores, uid never surfaced) is not "personal information … in identifiable form," notwithstanding ¶(11) combination in the operator's own records — i.e., does ¶(11) attach to the record as held or to the data as released?
2. If not: can we win "no release to a third party" for captain-approved family members, or should we abandon email-plus and launch with card-transaction consent (§312.5(b)(2)(ii)) at marginal cost?
3. Pre-consent flow: the child's uid reaches the backend while consent is pending. §312.5(c)(1) covers only *name or online contact information*; does the uid fit §312.5(c)(7) (SFIO-only) during pendency, and is our deletion-on-abandonment window defensible?
4. Firebase/Google posture: do our current terms make Google a "support for the internal operations" provider (Analytics/Ads fully off for child sessions), and do we hold §312.8(c) written assurances?
5. Should the direct notice (§312.4(c)(1)(iii)-(iv)) describe family visibility as a "potential opportunity for disclosure" — protective candor — without conceding it *is* third-party disclosure that forecloses email-plus? Exact drafting matters.
6. Would enrollment in an FTC-approved safe harbor (kidSAFE, ESRB Privacy Certified) — whose programs can approve consent methods under §312.5(b)(3) — buy pre-clearance of this exact posture?
7. Do the new §312.8(b) written security program and §312.10 written retention policy exist in signed form before launch?
8. Mixed-audience: does our age gate meet the amended §312.2 neutrality requirement (no default age, no encouragement to falsify)?

## Operator addendum (2026-08-27, engineering — not part of the analyst's memo)

The memo's fact pattern states the child's uid "never surfaces." That is true of the **UI**; at the **data layer** it is incomplete, and counsel should have the accurate wire-level facts:

- Family-readable Firestore documents carry the child's uid in data fields and document keys: the pending join-request row holds `{userId, userName, avatarId}` **in one document**; the invite document holds `toUserId` (and, since 2026-08-26, the same userName/avatarId identity stamp); post-admission, the membership document is keyed `families/{familyId}/members/{uid}`.
- Family members' devices therefore RECEIVE the uid combined with the username in released payloads, even though no screen renders the uid.

**Add to Question 1:** does "release of personal information … in identifiable form" reach protocol-level payload fields the recipient's client receives but the UI never renders — or is it measured by what is made available to the *person*? (A reading that counts every internal id in a payload would seem to make email-plus unusable for any multi-user feature by any operator, which cuts against it structurally — but it is the operator's soft spot as the wire format stands.)

**If counsel says data-layer release counts:** the engineering mitigation is de-identified family-readable projections — opaque request keys, uid stripped from released rows, identity carried only as the existing FR-86 snapshot stamp (username + stock avatar, no identifier). Feasible for the pre-admission approval surface; a larger refactor for post-admission membership documents. Specced as SRS v4 FR-108(e), not built pending counsel's answer.
